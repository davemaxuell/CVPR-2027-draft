## Operational shortlist

**K2** is fully specified in [proposal v1](../proposal_v1.md): train the existing predictor with native loss plus centered error of a frozen, qualified critic against measured per-action continuation values. Required signals are train-only actual branch returns, predicted features and legal instruction/history. Information flows to dynamics through the critic input, not its frozen parameters. This preserves instruction-independent physical dynamics while using an instruction-conditioned evaluator. It should help when prediction error reverses a meaningful action-value order; it fails if the order is unobservable, the scorer ignores futures, or its gradient rewards false features. An exact squared-gap/centered regression equivalence is a check, not a novelty claim. Deterministic DINO avoids claiming calibrated stochastic dynamics from a variance-penalizing sample loss.

**K3**, the simpler rival, uses actual observed next features `Z_a` and predictions `Zhat_a` with bounded nonnegative weights derived from repeat-mean utility differences:

`L = L_native + lambda sum_(a<b) w_ab ||(Zhat_a-Zhat_b)-(Z_a-Z_b)||^2`.

This removes the critic-gradient exploitation path. Its gradient is through the existing predictor only; labels and real features are constants. Uniform complete weights reduce to centered feature error; unequal weights couple action errors through a Laplacian. Common-mode errors still require the native anchor. Value gaps can reflect long continuation differences unrelated to local visual changes, so weighting need not target useful coordinates. It should help only if predictable local feature contrasts correlate with the measured ordering failure; it can hurt when large gaps weight irrelevant appearance or neglect difficult nearly tied actions. Compare uniform contrasts and per-action weighted native loss before interpreting any effect. DINO reuses cached real features; CDiT requires expensive differentiable decoding/encoding or a non-equivalent native-loss weighting. **If K3 matches or beats K2 at lower cost, replace K2; do not combine them to manufacture a second contribution.**

**K8 mathematical rejection.** For scalar critic `f(z)=z1*z2` and projector `P=diag(1,0)`, `P grad f=(z2,0)`. Its cross partials are 1 and 0, so it is not a scalar gradient field. More generally `P Hessian(f)` must be symmetric, requiring commutation with P. A legitimate alternative is the scalar `f(z_real+P(z-z_real))`, whose gradient is projected at that modified input, but it ignores orthogonal prediction errors and introduces a training-input difference. A native anchor does not establish that the retained span is sufficient for task value. VaGraM already motivates local real-state sensitivity. No evidence warrants this additional geometry or rank choice; it is discarded, not advertised as a new safe objective. A5 independently checked this reasoning after the candidate was shared.

## Why the chosen method is the smallest defensible candidate

The candidate adds no trainable module, inference search, language parser, representation ensemble, gate or second critic. It uses one objective extension after the existing scorer is qualified. Success/collision auxiliary heads are removed from the minimal training variant unless their original control demonstrates a distinct benefit. The eight-slot memory and two attention blocks remain existing reference components, not asserted necessities: compare with available policy history and simpler critic capacity to diagnose whether they earn their cost. Native-grid adaptation and base fallback are correctness/integration work, not contributions.

Every substantive addition has a removal test: lambda zero removes task-aware adaptation; per-action weighted native loss substitutes a simpler objective; no-future/current-copy/tiny-predictor models replace generation; history-only context tests extra memory; utility-only loss removes auxiliaries. Targeted coverage aggregation is added only on measured distribution shift. Preserving all trial names does not require training the full three-by-four grid before these gates.

## Contribution count and decision boundary

The candidate has **two possible research claims, zero established new CE results**: a consequential semantic-ordering finding, and a minimal outcome-based correction shown to resolve it. Their distinction is falsifiable: the finding can hold while the correction fails, and a generic correction gain without the predicted failure-specific pattern does not validate the finding. Nevertheless, a protocol itself is not a contribution and a routine task transfer may not clear the novelty threshold. The requested two strong novel contributions are therefore not currently secured. There is no third claim to add without diluting the argument.

The practical recommendation to be reconciled after independent review is **defer substantial investment pending the staged decisive evidence**, while implementing only replay/baseline/diagnostic prerequisites. This is not a rejection of prediction-assisted navigation or of the user's objective. It is also not a favorable novelty verdict inferred from a search that failed to find the exact intersection. Final reviewers receive the frozen candidate and raw evidence first, without this synthesis author's preferred verdict; they must independently evaluate correctness, novelty, feasibility and whether to proceed, defer or reject.

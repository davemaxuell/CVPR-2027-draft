# Remaining research gaps

**Cutoff: 22 October 2026.** These are bounded gaps in the reviewed evidence, not claims that a problem is universally unsolved. The final priority is P1, P2, then P4 as conditional empirical studies; P3 is rejected as an original-method proposal. A useful diagnostic can remain worthwhile even when the proposed model mechanism has close prior art.

| Gap | Precise setting and evidence | Strongest existing attempts and remaining distinction | Measurable success criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1: Recovery labels versus remaining requirements | A deviation reaches a familiar pose but the instruction's unfinished requirements depend on its history. CorrectNav/BudVLN geometry-based rejoining and the reviewed DUET teachers motivate inspecting labels; the finite paired-history example proves possibility, not natural prevalence. | FGR2R, M-TRACK, Landmark-RxR, LAW, HAMT's R2R-Back and reward-machine approaches are direct controls. The question is whether independent, alternative-aware labels reveal a useful residual error under matched data and actions. | Reliable independent annotation of natural teacher error, followed by improved recovery/ordered completion over the strongest matched teacher; no claim based only on synthetic counterexamples or endpoint SR. |
| G2: Validity versus navigator difficulty in data selection | Generated instruction–route pairs are accepted using model-relative compatibility or navigation performance. Neither quantity is an independent semantic truth label. RCM's failure example and RSI-Nav's acknowledged filter limits support the distinction. | Compatibility selection, APS, CCC, SRDF, RSI-Nav, Marky, GSA construction, cartography and clean-validation weighting already address quality or difficulty. The unresolved experiment is a fixed pool with independently audited validity and separate competence. | Lower invalid acceptance and valid-hard rejection at equal acceptance rate, then better held-out instruction following per accepted sample and per annotation effort than ordinary quality/difficulty selection. |
| G3: Attribution before persistent negative memory | An observed failure is compatible with recognition, approach/control or completion errors. PSG-Nav's outcome write path motivates auditing semantic negatives; the terminal-outcome example establishes non-identifiability without extra evidence. | PolarMem, HAM-VLN, Context-Nav, Memoir, CMMR, HiMeco and fault-attribution/memory work are mandatory precedents. Code-only corrections, source tags and generic reflection are not sufficient method novelty. | Fewer independently verified harmful semantic writes and later false suppressions than no-write/quarantine, witness-only and existing attribution controls, without unacceptable stale-memory costs under equal feedback and memory budgets. |
| G4: Ordered future-event information | Additive future feature expectations can merge distinct continuation distributions. Whether this matters for real NavQ-style targets and can be predicted from current observations is unestablished. | Successor/predictive-state representations, temporal logic, future QA, Semantic World Models, planning sufficiency and direct value/sequence predictors substantially constrain novelty. | A real, predictable candidate-ranking gap over equal-capacity single-event, history-only, sequence/future-QA and direct-value controls; improvements must survive continuation-policy shift and task-protocol matching. |
| G5: Value of additional evidence under a complete budget | Exploration, reinspection and selective reasoning can help but differ in new information, travel, calls and fallback rules. | ActiveVIG, ULN, Meta-Explore, SeekVLN, VerNav, CLOSER and DRIVE-Nav already supply strong acquisition/verification mechanisms. | A factorial comparison separates new views from extra reasoning on cached views and beats matched schedules while counting all episodes, rotations, return paths, latency and false decisions. |
| G6: Calibration of the event actually needed | Semantic novelty, action entropy, conformal answer inclusion, path retention and physical completion are different events. | EOE, KnowNo, Explore until Confident, CPNAV, VLA calibration and safety-gating methods provide adjacent guarantees or empirical scores with specific assumptions. | Clearly specified episode-level event/feedback units, valid calibration under the chosen protocol, and measured risk–coverage/cost under held-out scene and policy shifts. A calibrated proxy alone does not satisfy the criterion. |

The detailed evidence for G1–G2 and G5 is in [the language/recovery review](../followups/05_review.md), particularly matrix rows 05-D01–D09, R01–R11 and P01–P04. G3–G4 and G6 are developed in [the prediction/memory review](../followups/06_review.md), rows 06-M01–M05, W01–W06 and U01–U02. [The full matrix](../followups/Bottleneck_Matrix.csv) states comparability and limits for each chain. Newly discovered LAW/HiMeco and adjacent attribution overlaps must be judged through [the independent novelty review](../evaluations/09_novelty.md), not merely from their titles.

Prioritization uses five explicit questions: Does a concrete failure have credible evidence? Does it matter outside a contrived example? Is a substantive distinction left after the closest prior art? Can the distinction be tested cheaply and fairly? Would a positive or negative result change how researchers build or evaluate navigators? G1 initially scores best on a cheap falsifiable diagnostic; its method novelty is contingent. G2 has a clear controlled experiment but substantial annotation confounds. G3 matters for repeated deployment but requires correct failure labels and usable memory traces. G4 has the weakest present evidence of practical prevalence and remains speculative. G5–G6 are essential experimental disciplines and potential focused studies, but generic sensing/calibration proposals are already crowded.

The [final ranking](Final_Recommendation.md) reconciles the independent reviews and states the conditions for changing this ordering. No proposed learned method has been trained or benchmarked in this investigation.
